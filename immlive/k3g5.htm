EARLY PHASE OF ANAPHYLAXIS: IgE AND THEIR RECEPTORS

The IgE and other reagins display several properties, essential for the mechanism of anaphylaxis:

  • they are able to sensitize cells to allergens (cytophilyc or homocytotropic ability)
  • They do not activate the classic pathway of complement. (Very large amounts of immune complexes with IgE are able to activate alternative complement pathway under experimental conditions.
  • They can not permeate the intact placenta.

The cytophilyc ability is established by tight bound of these antibodies and special cellular receptors. Normally non-stimulated mastocytes and basophils have so called Fce -receptor of the  1st type    (Fce -R1), able to bind Fc-portion of reagin antibodies with high affinity and for a long term (for the whole longevity of these cells or up to 1 year!). Plasma membrane of every mast cell has about 500,000 molecules of Fce -R1. Besides this receptor, peculiar exclusively to mast cells and basophils, there is also another Fce -R2 receptor of low affinity. That type of reagin receptor normally exists on B-lymphocytes. In plus, it can be induced by IL-4, in course of anaphylaxis on several other cell types: eosinophils, monocytes, T-cells, platelets, alveolar macrophages. The patients with atopic dermatitis are genetically particular for Fce -R2 induction also on the surface of skin Langerhans’ cells. Fce -R1 is the main starting buttons for early phase of anaphylaxis. Fce -R2 is important for realization of its late phase and for resolution of anaphylactic inflammation.
