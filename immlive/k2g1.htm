PRIONIC INFECTIONS

The prions are the simplest among all exogenous pathogens. These pre-cellular obligate parasites are devoid of nucleic acids. They cause transmissive sponge encephalopathies in mammals and humans. Prionic particles are boiling resistant and proof against detergents. Their reproduction within host cells is possible due to presence of ubiquitous gene PrP in normal eukaryotic genome. PrP codes for the PrP-protein of 27-30 kD. By means of certain mechanism, which is not yet entirely understood, prions trigger the expression of this silent gene. The resulting PrP-protein, resistant to proteolysis, is cumulated within the cell, polymerized and forms a kind of amyloid (S. Prusiner, 1997; S. Flemming 1983). The amyloid plaques are deposited in brain tissue, especially involving hemispheres and cerebellum, and accompanied by local vacuolar degeneration, atrophy and astrogliosis. Several slow progredient CNS disorders have prionic etiology. They are transmitted via medical manipulations (neurosurgery, corneal grafts, and treatment with cadaver human pituitary preparations) and via enteric way (in consumption of infected meet products and in cannibalism).

D.C. Gaidusek

It was on Papua Island among cannibals of Fore tribe, that the diseases of this class have been initially described. At first these disorders were considered to be inborn metabolic errors, but later Nobel Prize winner of 1976 D.C. Gaidusek has proven the infectious origin of these illnesses. The problem of prionic infections has drawn the broad public attention since one of these maladies caused the death of prominent American choreographs G. Balanchine (1988). After this sad event it has been clear, that prionic pathology is not simply rare exotic bizarre or odd of Papua ethnography, but new class of everywhere spread infections. The prionic encephalopathy was found in ex-dwarves, many years ago "successfully" treated with growth hormone of cadaver origin.

The list of prionic diseases includes sheep's screpi disease, mad cow disease and several human illnesses: kuru disease and Kreutzfeld-Jacob disease. The last one has the incidence about 1:1000000 per year. Some other human prionopathies, like familiar sleepless disease, Alpers' syndrome and spino-cerebellar ataxia with dementia and amyloidosis (Herstmann-Schtreussler-Sheinier syndrome) occur much more rarely. All prionic disorders are known for very long latent period with subsequent rapid progressing of clinical manifestations (ataxia, dementia, myoclonus) and, finally, inevitable death. It is very characteristic, that immune system, in fact does not provide any defense against prions, being absolutely tolerant to them, which is proven by latent and progredient course of these diseases. Perhaps, this is resulted from ubiquitous character of prionic protein normal homologues. Moreover, immune response against prionic components of amyloid may contribute into amyloid composition and deposition (Amyloidosis).
