EARLY PHASE OF ANAPHYLAXIS: MAST CELLS' HETEROGENEITY

The main sources of anaphylactic autocoids – mast cells – have unitary histological origin, but settle in different tissues. Under the influence of their various tissue microenvironments, they acquire some significant differences. The basophils, although close to mast cells in their phenotype, descend from another bone marrow progenitor. There are 2 distinct subsets of mast cells: one belongs to mucosa membranes (MMM), another resides in connective tissue (CTM). The following table gives a comparis on of these 2 mast cell subsets. (What about the basophils, these cells contain much less histamine, proteoglycans and proteases than mastocytes, they are short-lived and synthesize chondroitin sulfates A and C).

Comparative Characterization of Two Mast Cell Subsets
Properties Mucosal mastocytes Connective Tissue mastocytes
Location Middle and distal gut mucosa, bronchial and lung mucosa, recirculate within MALT Connective tissue around nerves and blood vessels everywhere, especially in proximal part of gut and in skin.
Maturation control IL-3 from T cells Neuropeptides from diffuse endocrine system
Histamine & Proteases content Smaller Greater
Lipid autocoids production Mainly, leukotrienes Mainly, prostaglandins
Proteoglycans production Chiefly, chondroitin sulfates D and E Chiefly, heparin
Fce - R1 properties Amount is several thousand times greater Amount is 5000-8000 times smaller
Longevity and terms of sensitization Life time is shorter, provide more acute reaction Longer lifetime, more prolonged sensitization and inflammation.
