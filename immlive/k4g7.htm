DIRECT ACTIVATION OF SILENT AUTOREACTIVE T-HELPERS

The opposite action of two intracellular messengers – c-AMP and c-GMP – over lymphocyte functions is well-known (J. Hadden et al. 1979).

As it was proposed by Y. Schoenfeld and D. Isenberg (1989), the agents significantly increasing the content of c-AMP within helper T-cells may cause their direct activation, and thus overcome the clonal anergy of autoreactive lymphocytes. An example of this autoallergic mechanism in action is the pathogenesis of drug-induced warm immunohemolytic anemia. It can be provoked by methyl-DOPA (see above: warm hemolysins). Besides this drug, some other agents may sharply increase the activity of autoreactive T-clones, rising their c-AMP level (e.g. Bordetella pertussis’ toxin, bacterial superantigens and many drugs). Enhanced T-helper cell function manifested in the form of chronic hypersecretion of T-helper factors is seen in the lupus erythematosus-prone MRL-1pr/1pr mice, and a somewhat similar increase in helper cell activity is also noted in certain patients with SLE.
