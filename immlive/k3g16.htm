DIVERSITY OF DESTRUCTIVE CYTOTOXIC REACTIONS

Besides the autoallergic processes, elicited by intact self-antigens, some cytotoxic reactions may occur against alien cells (isoimmune reactions) and against changed self antigens or epitopes, created by complexing of self cells with alien haptens of antigens (heteroimmune reactions).

Antibody-mediated damage of blood cells causes the variety of hemocytopeniae. Cytotoxic reactions against RBC provoke hemolysis, creating many clinically significant disorders.

Hemolytic antibodies may be alloimmune (isoimmune) – when the targets are RBC of another individual, and autoimmune – if both RBC and Ig are produced by the same organism. Purely autoimmune are only that hemolytic anemiae, which involve intact self-antigens. These disorders are possible in derangement of self-tolerance. If the antibodies are directed towards pathological or altered RBC antigens (neoantigens, e.g. senescent cell marker or xenoantigens, or RBC absorbed drugs) in fact, are not autoimmune and have the mechanism non-distinguishable from that of alloimmune reactions. Previously they were traditionally designated as autoimmune hemolytic anemiae. To avoid any bafflement, we will use for such reactions the neologism “heteroimmune reactions”, reserving the term “autoimmune reactions” exclusively for processes aimed at intact self cells.

Alloimmune cytotoxic reactions are observed in transfusion of mismatched blood. RBC of incompatible donor is destroyed after being coated with recipient’s Ig against blood group antigens, absent on self-erythrocytes. There is an opinion that many common germs possess with similar antigens and, due to this, supposingly, prerequisite immunization by AB-agglutinogens is not necessary for transfusion reaction. The acting antibodies are IgM isohemagglutinins with high complement binding and opsonizing activities. Transfusion reactions are accompanied by RBC agglutination, microthrombosis, intravascular hemolysis, hyperbilirubinemia, hyperpotassiemia and even by hemolytic shock, close to allergic shock in its pathogenesis.

Rhesus incompatibility and sensitization of an Rh-negative mother by RBC from an Rh-positive baby cause hemolytic disease of the newborn or erythroblastosis fetalis. Approximately 80% of Caucasians express rhesus antigen on their RBC. Commonly Rh- individuals do not produce high titers of antibodies against most immunogenic D-epitope of Rh-antigen. But, during pregnancy of Rh- woman, carrying Rh+ baby, rhesus-sensitization of a mother is possible, especially if there is any placenta pathology with increase of placental barrier permeability. Ig G, produced in maternal organism, are able to penetrate across intact placenta, bind fetal RBC and activate complement. This may cause RBC destruction and erythroblastosis fetalis. Repeated pregnancy with Rh+ fetus is more dangerous for this syndrome. In Rh-conflict intracellular hemolysis is predominant, thus RBC are mostly destroyed in fetal spleen and liver. Pathological prolonged hemolytic jaundice, edema, and hepatosplenomegaly, sometimes - even bilirubin encephalopathy - may occur in neonates, affected by the disease. An effective precaution can be achieved by means of preventive anti-D-immunoglobulin injection to Rh- mothers immediately after birth of first Rh+ child. This method withdraws the Rh+ fetal RBC, crossed the placental barrier, before completion of sensitization process.

In congenital AB0-conflicts alloantibodies belong to IgM and do not cross an intact placental barrier, being captured in “placental shaft”. So, this type of maternal-fetal incompatibility can cause clinically significant disorder only if placenta is damaged. Maternal antibodies against fetal MHC antigens may cause also neonatal leukopeniae.
