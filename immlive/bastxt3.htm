Clonal Selection Theory

This theory was originally independently formulated in 1957 by M. Burnet in Ausiralia and D. Tolmage   in USA. Some parts of this concept nowadays are of purely historical interest. But main statements are still valid and accepted by the majority of immunologists with certain corrections.

  • First point was the following: each particular lymphocyte can recognize and respond for limited number of epitopes, probably – only one epitope. So, the whole lymphoid system has a mosaic composition and consists of great number of clones, each one maintaining between single and several thousand of antigen-specific cells, capable to produce recognizing molecules correspondent to appropriate antigens. The theory was developed before the discovery of T- and B- subsets. A. Shonberg and N.L. Warner have revealed this division of functions in lymphocytes as late as in 1962. But, as it has been shown later, clonal principles are applicable, although with minor stipulations, both for T- and B-cells. In modern immunology this is designated as “principle of sructural and genetic rigidity of mature lymphocyte’s receptors. Specific antigen “selects” appropriate clone, giving to it some advantage over the rivals in rate of proliferation – both directly and through cytokine signals. Irrelevant clones do not take part in activation process.
  • According to second key statement of this theory, the specificity of lymphoid clones is achieved before primary contact with antigen, owing to stochastic recombination of hyper-mutable genes in somatic mutation process. In the days of authors’ youth this pre-existence postulate looked enigmatic and even transcendental: it was emotionally difficult for a materialist to believe that immune system has known in advance about the structure of all possible antigens, a person may encounter on his or her pathways of fortune. But, if you remind that the structure of sequential antigenic determinants has the degree of variance quite far from endless (e.g., for proteins these are combinations out of 22 amino-acids taken by 5 or 6 of them) and the estimated number of lymphoid clones is about 100,000,000 (109), the task does not seem impossible. It is also necessary point out that the specificity of antibodies is relative and fitness between Ig and concrete antigen is compared with the correspondence of “key and keyhole” just for didactic purposes. Recently, it was demonstrated that the appropriate set of TcR in T-clones is developed in thymus, in parallels with:
  1. positive selection of the clones, capable to recognize the unique alien sequential epitopes in complex with self HLA-glycoproteins (I and II classes)
  2. negative selection or elimination of the clones, unable to react with self HLA-antigens (I and II classes)

This was predicted by the next postulate of clonal selection theory: the concept of innate autotolerance.

Lymphoid T-precursors, which are capable to respond on self unique sequential epitopes, complexed with self HLA-glycoproteins (I and II classes), or, in other words, potentially autoreactive ones, are also, in majority extinguished by intro-thymic negative selection. This process establishes relative innate autotolerance. Before the detailed discussion of autoimmune disorders, it is worth to underline that autoreactive B-cells do not take part in this or similar process of negative thymic selection. So, principally autotolerance is not absolute. Relative autotolerance is achieved mostly by clonal silence of autoreactive clones, not getting the proper anti-self T-helpers’ assistance. But this barrier can be bypassed in many situations (see Figures ). Moreover, it is vitally important for healthy immune system to keep some ability for natural autoimmunity: otherwise, the regulation by means of autoantiidiotypic antibodies, very essential for normal immunity, would not be possible.

Idiotype-Anti-Idiotype Networks and Regulation of Immune Response

All forms of immune response are controlled. The magnitude and qualities of any immune reaction depend on balance of all existing influences, those directed to its enlargement and also opposite ones, acting to its limitation. The major part of immunopathological disorders, like autoimmunity and allergy, are caused by derangement of regulation.

The basis of some regulation phenomena is genetic, related to MHC-products. But, environmental factors, diet, as well as age, sex, somatotype - are also essential. Great significance have the way of antigen invasion, physical state of antigen, its ability to persist within the cells, especially APC, for a long time, or absence of this property. The germs, surviving within phagocytes (Mycobacteria) and those killed by phagocyte (Staphylococci) cause different forms of immune reactions.

These difference may determine, what form of immune response would prevail: humoral (with B-cells and circulating Ig), or “cellular” (with T-clones). The same antigen is processed and presented by APC in different manner, depending on rout of its internalization, giving start to different kinds of immunologic phenomena through alternative classes of MHC-proteins.

Immunoregulation is mediated through various signals: antigens, autoantibodies, cytokines, and hormones.

The most fruitful approach in this field appeared to be network theory of immune regulation by Nobel Prize Laureate of 1984 Niels K. Jerne (Fig ).

According modern concept, immune response is mainly regulated by physiological autoimmunity. Jerne has developed network theory in order to explain this fact. This theory claims that every particular antigenic receptor of Ig idiotope, both on T- and on B-cells. is able to evoke the production of correspondent autoantiidiotypic antibodies, which, chiefly, decrease the expression of primary idiotypes.

To understand the relevance of idiotype-antiidiotype networks, we must once more recall that the antigen-combining sites of antibody molecules as well as T-cell receptors are composed of variable regions (or V domains) whose three-dimensional structure has a lock-and-key complementary to the antigenic determinants that they recognize. It is now well established that the variable regions of the T and B cell receptors themselves are "antigenic" and can therefore evoke the formation of antibodies directed against themselves. The antigenic determinants on the V domains are called idiotopes, a set of such idiotopes is unique idiotype of a given Ig or TcR, and the antibodies (or in living organism – autoantibodies) against idiotypes are, hence, antiidiotypic.

Practically, the system of antiidiotypic antibodies was for the first time experimentally obtained and characterized in the USSR, by L.N. Peunitzky, A.I. Fontalin and G.N. Kryzhanovsky as early as in 1960. They used the model of tetanus toxin and antitoxin. J. Oudin (1966) performed a bit later, similar research in France. If, as you can see in (Fig. ), an antigen X induce the genesis of anti-X antibodies (idiotypes), not only circulating Ig, but also B-cell clones, synthesizing them bear anti-X idiotypes. This is neoantigen for immune network, and it will stimulate another clone of B-cells to progress and make enough Ig towards anti-X, i.e. anti-idiotypes (anti-anti-X). A subsequent antibody response against the anti-idiotype, may theoretically result in the production of anti-anti-idiotypes. In fact, the network is not endless, but self-limited, because hypothetical anti-anti-anti-X are identical to anti-X (K.Rajevsky, A. Takemori, 1979).

If the anti-idiotypic antibody is directed precisely against the antigen-combining site (paratope) of the original antibody, the configuration of its own V-region will resemble the antigen (epitope) itself (Ab2b ), although anti-idiotypes directed against idiotopes irrelevant to paratope also may exist, and they do not resemble epitope (Ab2a ). Ab2b - anti-idiotypic antibody (being an internal immunologic image of the antigen – a term coined by J. Lindemann in 1979) could interact with the antigen-specific B-cell or T-cell and deliver a stimulatory or a suppressive signal. Thus, anti-idiotypic antibodies have the potential to regulate immune responses and are believed to perform such a function in vivo. Perturbations of the idiotype-anti-idiotype network can result in autoimmune diseases. Idiotype-antiidiotypic relations exist not only between antibodies and surface Ig, but also among T-cell receptors carried by different T-clones (G. Gaulton, M. Greene, 1989).

In fact, antiidiotypic autoantibodies belong to broad category of anti-receptor antibodies. If the primary antigen is a hormone (insulin, TSH, GH, FSH etc) - Ab2b mast be targeted against hormone receptor, and sometimes it displays a lock-and-key complementary fitness towards this receptor .

In some cases antiidiotypes are not prcise enough to imitate hormone and have to act not as “keys”, but as bad “picklocks”. The result may manifest in desensitization of receptor to hormone, or even in block of receptor-mediated process. The regulation of somatic cells growth and functions by the autoantibodies, presenting the internal immunological image of a hormone, has been several times clearly demonstrated for many cells, both in vivo and in vitro ( Farid-Nadir, D. Linticum, 1989; G. Gaulton, M. Greene, 1986, A. Zaichik, L.Churilov, A. Vassil’ev et al, 1989, 1991). The brightest example is the pathogenesis of Graves’ disease, in which both hyperthyroidism and goiter are produced by autoantiidiotypic antibodies against TSH, functional agonists of thyroid cell TSH-receptors. There are also some data, proving that not only surface recognition molecules, but also regulating cis-elements of chromatin are involved in antiidiotypic network, justifying the brave proposal of Dr. Albert Sh. Zaichik, that antiidiotypic Ig may be of some relation with the direct regulation of gene expression within living cell nuclei. Intracellular penetration of such anti-chromatin Ig has been clearly demonstrated on various living cells in vivo and in vitro ( D. Alarcon-Segovia et al., 1973-1979, A. Zaichik, L.Churilov, A. Vasiliev et al, 1973-1991, M. Tsuneoka et al, 1988).

Thus, according to network theory, immune system is able to elaborate functionally active or competitive antagonistic informational copies of many endogenous and exogenous regulators, and not only those of protein nature. The imitation (or block) of hormone, neurotransmitter, growth factor, and even drug effects by means of antiidiotypes has been demonstrated in vivo and in vitro (E.M. Smith, J.E. Blalock, 1986; Y. Shechter, 1988, A. Strosberg et al., 1983). The striking example of this kind is production of monoclonal antiidiotypic antibodies against haloperidole, able to imitate drug action over D2-dopaminergic receptor (Z. Elazar et al, 1988).

The authors of this program predict in the nearest future the possibility to create opiate-imitating antiidiotypes and proclaim an idea to treat (or may be, even prevent by vaccination) the narcotic addiction, with the help of these approach.

The Effector Component of Immune System

The main effectors of immune response are complement system, phagocytes and cytotoxic cells. They provide the tools for target destruction and/or elimination, directed by recognizing elements – antibodies and TcRs.

Complement System

By the end of the 19th century T. Nattell and later J. Phagocytosis

The phagocytosis is one of the most impressive discoveries in the Pathological Physiology of XIX century. This phenomenon was originally described in 1883 by Elia I. Mechnikoff, Nobel Prize winner of 1908.

According to definition, given 100 years later, phagocytosis is receptor-mediated endocytosis of the objects greater than 1 mcm, by various living cells, with compalsory use of microfilaments (Z. Cohn, 1983) .

The heros of phagocytosis – phagocytes. Mechnikoff wrote about macrophages and microphages. In 1912 A. Shilling revealed, that former are monocytes and their tissue descendants, and later are different granulocytes. In 1924 L. Aschoff proposed the concept of reticulo-endothelial system (RES), joining all functionally related endothelial and phagocytic cells (both settled and nomadic). Later it was proven that the promonocytes of bone marrow, transitory migrating in periferal blood monocytes, as well as their versatile posterity within different tissues ( alveolar, peritoneal, pleural, pericardial, synovial macrophages, Kupffer’s cells of liver, microglial elements of CNS, histiocytes of connective tissue, osteoclasts of the bones, epitelioid skin cells etc.) have singular origin, close functions and represent the system of mononuclear phagocytes (A. Lesser, 1983). If all the members of a noble genus have the same origin (like Romanovs) and the same occupations (Tsars) – people call this Dynasty.

Thus, mononuclear phagocytes represent a dynasty of phagocyting cells!

Since 1972 WHO recommended to withdraw the term RES because it included the cells, not belonging to above mentioned dynasty and due to weak or not proven character of endothelium phagocytic abilities. But, the current data on the cell adhesion molecules and tight adhesive relations (mutual co-activation) of phagocytes and endothelial cells witness for great astuteness, displayed by L.Aschoff, and Pathology now tends to re-appreciate the RES concept.

So, phagocytosis is realised by mononuclear dynasty cells and by various phagocytes of myeloid origin, mostly neutrophils. Other granulocytes are less active as phagocytes. It is now clear, that platelets also serve as microphages, morover, their ability to phagocytosis is greatle activated by alpha-fetoprotein (H. Kemona et al., 1990). Mature lymphocytes do not phagocyte. Occasionally, many other cells, like fibroblasts, smooth myocytes and even epitheliocytes perform the acts of phagocytosis (I.V. Davydovsky, 1969), but only and exclusively professional phagocytes (mononuclear and polymorphonuclear) are capable for immune-mediated phagocytosis (V.L. Bykov, 1998).

The process of phagocytosis has 4 distinct stages:

  • Approaching of a phagocyte to the object is achieved by means of positive chemotaxis which is locomotion oriented alond the gradient of certain substance. The mechanism of this phenomenon is established by chemoattractant receptors, which bind and smell the attracting substance. Increase of intracellular calcium concentration after the occupation of the receptors on anterior pole of a phagocyte causes reversible gelatination of cell cytoplasm and puts cytoskeleton into function. The cell prostrate the lamellipod oriented along the gradient of a smelled attractant. The phagocyte moves to the side of greater attractant concentration as a “car with front-wheel drive” (R. Gotran et al, 1989). In immune responses, antibodies or immune complexes, as well as components of complement, bacterial products or decay products of damaged tissues may serve as chemoattractants, because phagocytes have the receptors for them.
  • Attachment of a phagocyte to the object is achieved by direct bound of chemoattractant molecules (if they are on the surface of an object) – and their receptors. This process is called opsonisation, and the surface-located chemoattractants are named opsonins. IgM and IgG, as well as complement factors, first of all C3b, are potent opsonins. Opsonization triggers the mechanisms of phagocyte activation ( respiratory burst, active oxygen radicals (AOR) and eicosanoids production) long before the object gets inside.
  • Engulfment or internalization of an object is a kind of receptor endocytosis.                                                                   Ig trigger it immediately on attachment, but C3b requires aditional bound of fibronectin either lamellin proteins by special receptor of phagocytes and/or cytokine stimulus. Anyway, the process picture seems like the function of zip-fastener, with object sliding into the cell along the panels of opsonin receptors. Microfilaments are pivotal for this process, which requires much more energy, than other forms of endocytosis, e.g. pinocytosis. The products of Ig Fc-portion degradation – leukokinins (tetrapeptide taftsin and pentapeptide rigin) are able to accelerate chemokinesis (non-directed locomotion of WBC), attachnent and engulfment. Some toxic bactericide products ( AOR) are secreted by phagocyte and dissipated around during engulfment. This kind of exocytosis is called by R. Gotran at al: “ regurgitation during feeding”.
  • Degradation or, in classic works, digestion – is a conclusive stage of phagocytosis, not always achievable. It is produced after transportation of phagosome with an object inside it to the lysosomes and peroxysomes. The fusion of these organoids creates phagolysosome, the arena of last mortal combat. The leading mechanism of object degradation in phagocytosis is oxygen-dependent one, achieved by active oxygen and galogenic radicals, produced within phagocytes (B.M. Babior, 1982). An additional role belongs to non-oxygen dependent mechanisms, including lysosomal hydrolysis (Ch. De Duve, 1973).

Polymorphonuclears have greater radical production (for example, produce perchlorate bleach, not fabricated in mononuclear phagocytes due to absense of myeloperoxidase. But, their antioxidant systems are weaker, than in macrophages. So, neutrophil is, in fact, disposable cell, or used as weapon like hand grenade in inflammation, cause it does not sustain its own oxidative strike. Dead neutrophils are main constituents of the pus. The macrophages are able to survive, and, due to this, macrophagal phagocytosis is of much greater and broader consequences for immune response, than neutrophilic one.

The polymorphonuclear phagocytes, chiefly, are pure effectors of immune response. But macrophages ( and – even more- close to them dendritic cells) are not only effectors, but also regulators of immune reactions.

The regulatory role of macrophages for immunity is related to several aspects:

  • Phagocytosis of an antigen is a first step of its processing and presentation
  • Activation of macrophages in phagocytosis causes production of interleukin-1 and other cytokines, which start acute phase response and fever.
  • During and after phagocytosis macrophages produce and secret autocoids of pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory action, which may determine the duration and result of the inflammation. The directly take part in regulation of fibroplasia and tissue reparation.
  • If the pphagocytosis was not completed and the object persists within macrophages and APCs, this causes and maintains the delayed hypersensitivity reactions and phenomenon of granoloma formation, known in many infectious and some non-infectious diseases.

In this brief synopsis of phagocytosis we have seen that this ancient defensive archetype, known in primitive unicellular organisms, is effectively embedded as a block into the building of immune response, invented and constructed by evolution much later. Another side of this “ opportunistic usage of old means in new situation” is negative one. Unicellular beings can eject the remnants of victim and toxic autocoids – and get rid of them. But in multicellular organism, phagocytosis of antibody-coated objects and immune complexes by leukocytes drawn in by the chemotactic factors results in the release or generation of a variety of additional proinflammatory substances, including prostaglandins, vasodilator peptides, and chemotactic substances, as well as several lysosomal enzymes, including proteases capable of digesting basement membrane, collagen, elastin, and cartilage. They spread over adjacent tissue and involve it in secondary alteration. So, phagocytosis is potent, but not selective weapon, and its usefulness is relative.

Tissue damage may also be mediated by free oxygen radicals produced by activated phagocytes.

Mechanisms of cell cytotoxicity

Besides phagocytosis, which establishes intracellular mechanism of killing, immune response involves alternative methods of target cell alteration, without its engulfment. These weapons are known as “cell-mediated cytotoxicity”, commonly divided into antibody-dependent and antibody – independent kinds. Not only tumor cells, but also graft cells, infected cells and some bacterial cells, as well as intact self-targets of autoallergy may suffer from cellular cytotoxicity.

The ways of target recognition and assassination during cytotoxicity broadly vary, and the same cell may use different ones in combination. Briefly, the target cell can be killed either by means of “break” or through giving of imperative suicidal “order”. An additional role in cell attachment during cytotoxicity belongs to surface adhesion molecules.

This phenomenon is realized by T-killers, by various lymphoid and non-lymphoid elements traditionally referred to as K-cells and also by natural killers (known as NK-cells or null-cells).

T-killers take part in HLA-restricted antibody-independent cytotoxicity. Most of them are CD8+ and recognize their targets in the context of HLA class I proteins. Minor subset of T-killers, essential for graft rejection, is CD4-positive and respond in context of Class II HLA proteins. Clonal Selection Theory

This theory was originally independently formulated in 1957 by M. Burnet in Ausiralia and D. Tolmage   in USA. Some parts of this concept nowadays are of purely historical interest. But main statements are still valid and accepted by the majority of immunologists with certain corrections.

  • First point was the following: each particular lymphocyte can recognize and respond for limited number of epitopes, probably – only one epitope. So, the whole lymphoid system has a mosaic composition and consists of great number of clones, each one maintaining between single and several thousand of antigen-specific cells, capable to produce recognizing molecules correspondent to appropriate antigens. The theory was developed before the discovery of T- and B- subsets. A. Shonberg and N.L. Warner have revealed this division of functions in lymphocytes as late as in 1962. But, as it has been shown later, clonal principles are applicable, although with minor stipulations, both for T- and B-cells. In modern immunology this is designated as “principle of sructural and genetic rigidity of mature lymphocyte’s receptors. Specific antigen “selects” appropriate clone, giving to it some advantage over the rivals in rate of proliferation – both directly and through cytokine signals. Irrelevant clones do not take part in activation process.
  • According to second key statement of this theory, the specificity of lymphoid clones is achieved before primary contact with antigen, owing to stochastic recombination of hyper-mutable genes in somatic mutation process. In the days of authors’ youth this pre-existence postulate looked enigmatic and even transcendental: it was emotionally difficult for a materialist to believe that immune system has known in advance about the structure of all possible antigens, a person may encounter on his or her pathways of fortune. But, if you remind that the structure of sequential antigenic determinants has the degree of variance quite far from endless (e.g., for proteins these are combinations out of 22 amino-acids taken by 5 or 6 of them) and the estimated number of lymphoid clones is about 100,000,000 (109), the task does not seem impossible. It is also necessary point out that the specificity of antibodies is relative and fitness between Ig and concrete antigen is compared with the correspondence of “key and keyhole” just for didactic purposes. Recently, it was demonstrated that the appropriate set of TcR in T-clones is developed in thymus, in parallels with:
  1. positive selection of the clones, capable to recognize the unique alien sequential epitopes in complex with self HLA-glycoproteins (I and II classes)
  2. negative selection or elimination of the clones, unable to react with self HLA-antigens (I and II classes)

This was predicted by the next postulate of clonal selection theory: the concept of innate autotolerance.

Lymphoid T-precursors, which are capable to respond on self unique sequential epitopes, complexed with self HLA-glycoproteins (I and II classes), or, in other words, potentially autoreactive ones, are also, in majority extinguished by intro-thymic negative selection. This process establishes relative innate autotolerance. Before the detailed discussion of autoimmune disorders, it is worth to underline that autoreactive B-cells do not take part in this or similar process of negative thymic selection. So, principally autotolerance is not absolute. Relative autotolerance is achieved mostly by clonal silence of autoreactive clones, not getting the proper anti-self T-helpers’ assistance. But this barrier can be bypassed in many situations (see Figures ). Moreover, it is vitally important for healthy immune system to keep some ability for natural autoimmunity: otherwise, the regulation by means of autoantiidiotypic antibodies, very essential for normal immunity, would not be possible.

Idiotype-Anti-Idiotype Networks and Regulation of Immune Response

All forms of immune response are controlled. The magnitude and qualities of any immune reaction depend on balance of all existing influences, those directed to its enlargement and also opposite ones, acting to its limitation. The major part of immunopathological disorders, like autoimmunity and allergy, are caused by derangement of regulation.

The basis of some regulation phenomena is genetic, related to MHC-products. But, environmental factors, diet, as well as age, sex, somatotype - are also essential. Great significance have the way of antigen invasion, physical state of antigen, its ability to persist within the cells, especially APC, for a long time, or absence of this property. The germs, surviving within phagocytes (Mycobacteria) and those killed by phagocyte (Staphylococci) cause different forms of immune reactions.

These difference may determine, what form of immune response would prevail: humoral (with B-cells and circulating Ig), or “cellular” (with T-clones). The same antigen is processed and presented by APC in different manner, depending on rout of its internalization, giving start to different kinds of immunologic phenomena through alternative classes of MHC-proteins.

Immunoregulation is mediated through various signals: antigens, autoantibodies, cytokines, and hormones.

The most fruitful approach in this field appeared to be network theory of immune regulation by Nobel Prize Laureate of 1984 Niels K. Jerne (Fig ).

According modern concept, immune response is mainly regulated by physiological autoimmunity. Jerne has developed network theory in order to explain this fact. This theory claims that every particular antigenic receptor of Ig idiotope, both on T- and on B-cells. is able to evoke the production of correspondent autoantiidiotypic antibodies, which, chiefly, decrease the expression of primary idiotypes.

To understand the relevance of idiotype-antiidiotype networks, we must once more recall that the antigen-combining sites of antibody molecules as well as T-cell receptors are composed of variable regions (or V domains) whose three-dimensional structure has a lock-and-key complementary to the antigenic determinants that they recognize. It is now well established that the variable regions of the T and B cell receptors themselves are "antigenic" and can therefore evoke the formation of antibodies directed against themselves. The antigenic determinants on the V domains are called idiotopes, a set of such idiotopes is unique idiotype of a given Ig or TcR, and the antibodies (or in living organism – autoantibodies) against idiotypes are, hence, antiidiotypic.

Practically, the system of antiidiotypic antibodies was for the first time experimentally obtained and characterized in the USSR, by L.N. Peunitzky, A.I. Fontalin and G.N. Kryzhanovsky as early as in 1960. They used the model of tetanus toxin and antitoxin. J. Oudin (1966) performed a bit later, similar research in France. If, as you can see in (Fig. ), an antigen X induce the genesis of anti-X antibodies (idiotypes), not only circulating Ig, but also B-cell clones, synthesizing them bear anti-X idiotypes. This is neoantigen for immune network, and it will stimulate another clone of B-cells to progress and make enough Ig towards anti-X, i.e. anti-idiotypes (anti-anti-X). A subsequent antibody response against the anti-idiotype, may theoretically result in the production of anti-anti-idiotypes. In fact, the network is not endless, but self-limited, because hypothetical anti-anti-anti-X are identical to anti-X (K.Rajevsky, A. Takemori, 1979).

If the anti-idiotypic antibody is directed precisely against the antigen-combining site (paratope) of the original antibody, the configuration of its own V-region will resemble the antigen (epitope) itself (Ab2b ), although anti-idiotypes directed against idiotopes irrelevant to paratope also may exist, and they do not resemble epitope (Ab2a ). Ab2b - anti-idiotypic antibody (being an internal immunologic image of the antigen – a term coined by J. Lindemann in 1979) could interact with the antigen-specific B-cell or T-cell and deliver a stimulatory or a suppressive signal. Thus, anti-idiotypic antibodies have the potential to regulate immune responses and are believed to perform such a function in vivo. Perturbations of the idiotype-anti-idiotype network can result in autoimmune diseases. Idiotype-antiidiotypic relations exist not only between antibodies and surface Ig, but also among T-cell receptors carried by different T-clones (G. Gaulton, M. Greene, 1989).

In fact, antiidiotypic autoantibodies belong to broad category of anti-receptor antibodies. If the primary antigen is a hormone (insulin, TSH, GH, FSH etc) - Ab2b mast be targeted against hormone receptor, and sometimes it displays a lock-and-key complementary fitness towards this receptor .

In some cases antiidiotypes are not prcise enough to imitate hormone and have to act not as “keys”, but as bad “picklocks”. The result may manifest in desensitization of receptor to hormone, or even in block of receptor-mediated process. The regulation of somatic cells growth and functions by the autoantibodies, presenting the internal immunological image of a hormone, has been several times clearly demonstrated for many cells, both in vivo and in vitro ( Farid-Nadir, D. Linticum, 1989; G. Gaulton, M. Greene, 1986, A. Zaichik, L.Churilov, A. Vassil’ev et al, 1989, 1991). The brightest example is the pathogenesis of Graves’ disease, in which both hyperthyroidism and goiter are produced by autoantiidiotypic antibodies against TSH, functional agonists of thyroid cell TSH-receptors. There are also some data, proving that not only surface recognition molecules, but also regulating cis-elements of chromatin are involved in antiidiotypic network, justifying the brave proposal of Dr. Albert Sh. Zaichik, that antiidiotypic Ig may be of some relation with the direct regulation of gene expression within living cell nuclei. Intracellular penetration of such anti-chromatin Ig has been clearly demonstrated on various living cells in vivo and in vitro ( D. Alarcon-Segovia et al., 1973-1979, A. Zaichik, L.Churilov, A. Vasiliev et al, 1973-1991, M. Tsuneoka et al, 1988).

Thus, according to network theory, immune system is able to elaborate functionally active or competitive antagonistic informational copies of many endogenous and exogenous regulators, and not only those of protein nature. The imitation (or block) of hormone, neurotransmitter, growth factor, and even drug effects by means of antiidiotypes has been demonstrated in vivo and in vitro (E.M. Smith, J.E. Blalock, 1986; Y. Shechter, 1988, A. Strosberg et al., 1983). The striking example of this kind is production of monoclonal antiidiotypic antibodies against haloperidole, able to imitate drug action over D2-dopaminergic receptor (Z. Elazar et al, 1988).

The authors of this program predict in the nearest future the possibility to create opiate-imitating antiidiotypes and proclaim an idea to treat (or may be, even prevent by vaccination) the narcotic addiction, with the help of these approach.

Phagocytosis

The phagocytosis is one of the most impressive discoveries in the Pathological Physiology of XIX century. This phenomenon was originally described in 1883 by Elia I. Mechnikoff, Nobel Prize winner of 1908.

According to definition, given 100 years later, phagocytosis is receptor-mediated endocytosis of the objects greater than 1 mcm, by various living cells, with compalsory use of microfilaments (Z. Cohn, 1983) .

The heros of phagocytosis – phagocytes. Mechnikoff wrote about macrophages and microphages. In 1912 A. Shilling revealed, that former are monocytes and their tissue descendants, and later are different granulocytes. In 1924 L. Aschoff proposed the concept of reticulo-endothelial system (RES), joining all functionally related endothelial and phagocytic cells (both settled and nomadic). Later it was proven that the promonocytes of bone marrow, transitory migrating in periferal blood monocytes, as well as their versatile posterity within different tissues ( alveolar, peritoneal, pleural, pericardial, synovial macrophages, Kupffer’s cells of liver, microglial elements of CNS, histiocytes of connective tissue, osteoclasts of the bones, epitelioid skin cells etc.) have singular origin, close functions and represent the system of mononuclear phagocytes (A. Lesser, 1983). If all the members of a noble genus have the same origin (like Romanovs) and the same occupations (Tsars) – people call this Dynasty.

Thus, mononuclear phagocytes represent a dynasty of phagocyting cells!

Since 1972 WHO recommended to withdraw the term RES because it included the cells, not belonging to above mentioned dynasty and due to weak or not proven character of endothelium phagocytic abilities. But, the current data on the cell adhesion molecules and tight adhesive relations (mutual co-activation) of phagocytes and endothelial cells witness for great astuteness, displayed by L.Aschoff, and Pathology now tends to re-appreciate the RES concept.

So, phagocytosis is realised by mononuclear dynasty cells and by various phagocytes of myeloid origin, mostly neutrophils. Other granulocytes are less active as phagocytes. It is now clear, that platelets also serve as microphages, morover, their ability to phagocytosis is greatle activated by alpha-fetoprotein (H. Kemona et al., 1990). Mature lymphocytes do not phagocyte. Occasionally, many other cells, like fibroblasts, smooth myocytes and even epitheliocytes perform the acts of phagocytosis (I.V. Davydovsky, 1969), but only and exclusively professional phagocytes (mononuclear and polymorphonuclear) are capable for immune-mediated phagocytosis (V.L. Bykov, 1998).

The process of phagocytosis has 4 distinct stages:

  • Approaching of a phagocyte to the object is achieved by means of positive chemotaxis which is locomotion oriented alond the gradient of certain substance. The mechanism of this phenomenon is established by chemoattractant receptors, which bind and smell the attracting substance. Increase of intracellular calcium concentration after the occupation of the receptors on anterior pole of a phagocyte causes reversible gelatination of cell cytoplasm and puts cytoskeleton into function. The cell prostrate the lamellipod oriented along the gradient of a smelled attractant. The phagocyte moves to the side of greater attractant concentration as a “car with front-wheel drive” (R. Gotran et al, 1989). In immune responses, antibodies or immune complexes, as well as components of complement, bacterial products or decay products of damaged tissues may serve as chemoattractants, because phagocytes have the receptors for them.
  • Attachment of a phagocyte to the object is achieved by direct bound of chemoattractant molecules (if they are on the surface of an object) – and their receptors. This process is called opsonisation, and the surface-located chemoattractants are named opsonins. IgM and IgG, as well as complement factors, first of all C3b, are potent opsonins.   • Activation of macrophages in phagocytosis causes production of interleukin-1 and other cytokines, which start acute phase response and fever.

In this brief synopsis of phagocytosis we have seen that this ancient defensive archetype, known in primitive unicellular organisms, is effectively embedded as a block into the building of immune response, invented and constructed by evolution much later. Another side of this “ opportunistic usage of old means in new situation” is negative one. Unicellular beings can eject the remnants of victim and toxic autocoids – and get rid of them. But in multicellular organism, phagocytosis of antibody-coated objects and immune complexes by leukocytes drawn in by the chemotactic factors results in the release or generation of a variety of additional proinflammatory substances, including prostaglandins, vasodilator peptides, and chemotactic substances, as well as several lysosomal enzymes, including proteases capable of digesting basement membrane, collagen, elastin, and cartilage. They spread over adjacent tissue and involve it in secondary alteration. So, phagocytosis is potent, but not selective weapon, and its usefulness is relative.

Tissue damage may also be mediated by free oxygen radicals produced by activated phagocytes.

Mechanisms of cell cytotoxicity

Besides phagocytosis, which establishes intracellular mechanism of killing, immune response involves alternative methods of target cell alteration, without its engulfment. These weapons are known as “cell-mediated cytotoxicity”, commonly divided into antibody-dependent and antibody – independent kinds. Not only tumor cells, but also graft cells, infected cells and some bacterial cells, as well as intact self-targets of autoallergy may suffer from cellular cytotoxicity.

The ways of target recognition and assassination during cytotoxicity broadly vary, and the same cell may use different ones in combination. Briefly, the target cell can be killed either by means of “break” or through giving of imperative suicidal “order”. An additional role in cell attachment during cytotoxicity belongs to surface adhesion molecules.

This phenomenon is realized by T-killers, by various lymphoid and non-lymphoid elements traditionally referred to as K-cells and also by natural killers (known as NK-cells or null-cells).

T-killers take part in HLA-restricted antibody-independent cytotoxicity. Most of them are CD8+ and recognize their targets in the context of HLA class I proteins. Minor subset of T-killers, essential for graft rejection, is CD4-positive and respond in context of Class II HLA proteins. Upon recognition, they produce a process, figuratively called “Judas’ kiss” (Fig. ).

In this process, the T-killer binds avidly and specifically to its target cell and then delivers a lytic signal. The biochemical basis of the "lethal hit" delivered by T cells is under intense scrutiny, as an understanding of this process may allow the development of therapeutic agents that prevent T cell-mediated tissue damage in conditions such as graft regection. Currently three mechanisms of T-cell cytotoxicity (acting singly or in concert) are being investigated.

Macrophages, NK- and T-killers are able to treat target cells with lethal cytokines, like lymphotoxin, cachexin and cytotoxic NK-factor. These signals are not lytic per se, but able to trigger the intracellular program of suicide – apoptosis, which is some kind of programmed cellular self-disassembly, with minimal possible dissipation of inflammation mediators. The apoptosis also involves calcium ion, but does not depend on perforin effect and is regarded in immunology as “postponed killing”. IFNg is potent synergist of these agents, blocking the nucleic biosynthesis in target cells. There are also other, calcium- and perforin-independent mechanisms of cytotoxicity.

It should be remembered that T-mediated cytolysis is highly specific and that adjacent ("innocent bystander") cells are not damaged. Critical to such specificity is the ability of T-killers to recognize the cells bearing the appropriate target antigens. It is now abundantly clear that MHC class I antigens play an extremely important role in the process by which CD8+ T-cells recognized their target cells. NK cells are found in the peripheral blood and the lymphoid tissues of man as well as a variety of animal species. Special receptor of NK-cell binds still unknown ligands of target cell. This effect is used in biotechnological treatment of malignancies. NK cells utilize two distinct mechanisms to lyse their target cells. One, belonging to antibody- dependent cellular cytotoxicity, utilizes NK cell surface Fc receptors and antibody directed against the target - cell antigens. The other involves direct interaction between NK cells and their target cells and utilizes above - mentioned NK cell receptors that have not yet been fully characterized.
