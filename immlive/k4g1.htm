PHYSIOLOGICAL AUTOIMMUNITY AND IMMUNOLOGIC SELF TOLERANCE

For a long time, in fact, since the moment of its origination, classic immunology, regarding the problem of autoimmunity, insisted on the “ horror autotoxicus” thesis, first formulated by P. Ehrlich and E. Morgenroth (1900-1901). This concept claims that no antibodies can be formed against body’s own components in healthy organism.

Almost immediately after it, Effim S. London (1901) and P. Ulengut (1903) have obtained under experimental condition autoantibodies against sperm and lens, respectively. Than K. Landsteiner and J. Donat have described first autoimmune human disease – cold immunohemolytic anemia (see above). So, the paradigm was waved, but not broken: still the immunologists believed that autoimmune response is illegal and possible only in experiments and in diseases. This concept with minor changes was still in use until late 70ies of XX century. Attentive look on the works published by P. Ehrlich shows, that this classic of immunology did not exclude the possibility of physiological autoimmune response. I.I. Metchnikov and E. Roux in their memorial article "L'ouvres de Paul Ehrlich" (1914) have quoted Ehrlich as follows: “The cellular receptors of different affinity play an important role in pathology. The nutrients affix to them and penetrate into the cell via them. Among these receptors, those complementary to poisons introduce the toxins into the cell, which may, due to this, get a disease or even perish. If a cell survives, it produces new receptors… owing to certain reaction, not only lost, but also new receptors are produced in greater amount. Copious receptors disjoined from cell surface, transfer into the fluids, ready to react with target substance (toxin). Anti-toxic serum is a serum, containing excessive receptors, disjoined off the cells, and absorbed a toxin. This fact determines its specificity. P. Ehrlich predicted the homology between cell receptors and autoantibodies, which was proven much later (A. Kuhlberg, 1986).

At bottom of fact, as double stranded DNA encodes genetic programs of an organism, it is obvious that just opposite to any peptide its structural antonym or anti-peptide is encoded. J. E. Blalock et al. (1986, 1991) demonstrated this fact for the genes of several peptide hormones and their receptors. It means that the ability to generate anti-signals and anti-receptors is deep-seated into the intrinsic fundament of cell regulation.

Current concept of autoimmunity is inherited from another founder of Immunology – I.I. Metchnikov, who hypothesized that moderate degree of autoimmune response is physiological. According to this view, formulated in details to the middle of 80-ies some degree of autoimmunity is natural phenomenon, mandatory for normal function of immune system itself and, moreover, used for normal regulation and synchronization somatic cell functions and morphogenetic processes (Ch. Janeway, 1982; B. Donter, 1991). Normally autoimmunity is limited by many regulation mechanisms. Under pathological conditions this regulation may be deranged, and autoallergy (or autoimmune disorders – which is the same) is provoked. Hence autoimmunity and autoallergy are not synonyms (H.R. Smith, A.D. Steinberg, 1983). The mechanisms of self-tolerance already were discussed in “Basic Immunology” section. Here we just would like to remind: although each particular T or B lymphocyte has the surface receptor correspondent to a single unique epitope, different lymphocytes hold different receptors. It seems that every possible epitope may find among this diversity a receptor, which is relatively reciprocal to its structure. This is established in course of T and B cells differentiation by means of alternative transcription and splicing of hypervariable multiallelic genes coded for some portions of TcR and Ig. As this variance is sporadic, it is not surprising that 15-20% of resulting lymphoid clones convey autoreactive surface TcR and Ig molecules.

Several mechanisms, albeit not well understood, have been postulated to explain the tolerant state. Three of these are worth consideration: clonal deletion, clonal anergy and suppression of autoreactive lymphocytes.

The main tasks of self-toleration are achieved while lymphocytes are in immature status. As early as in 1938 H. Traub has shown that in antenatal viral infection immune system gets tolerant of the infect. Later (1945) R.D. Owen observed “an experiment of nature” – dizygotic cattle twins fused at the placentae (Fig ). After separation each twin was permanently tolerant of the other’s RBC antigens.

Resuming all similar data, P. Medawar, M. Burnet and F. Fenner (1944, 1949) have proposed a hypothesis of early prenatal toleration of lymphocytes by contact with self-antigens. In 1953 this concept has got experimental confirmation from the works of R. E. Billingham, L. Brent, R. Hasek and P. Medawar (last author was later rewarded with Nobel Prize). In these experiments Medawar’s group induced tolerance of skin allografts by means of neonatal injection of allogeneic cells to graft recipients.

J. Miller (1966), who discovered the role of thymus in T-lymphocytes differentiation, enriched the concept of antenatal tolerance. Later A. Allison et al. (1971) have proven the existence of independent T and B cell toleration, with significantly lower threshold of toleration for T-cells. At the same time, J. Lederberg has fairly argued that due to somatic mutation the enrichment of immune cell diversity has to continue through the whole postnatal life. So, if tolerance mechanisms were limited with antenatal period, they for sure would be insufficient. Newly established autoreactive mutant clones also mast be controlled. That’s why in the history of self tolerance studies the discovery, made by G. Nossal and B. Pike (1980), stands very high. These authors reported that antenatal clonal deletion is supplemented by postnatal clonal anergy, induced by contact of immature T precursors with an exuberance of self-antigens. So, an important correction of initial concept is that not stage of individual onthogenesis, but a stage of concrete T-lymphocyte differentiation at the moment of its contact with an antigen is pivotal for toleration.

Nevertheless, the autoantigens expressed exclusively in mature age and late ontogenesis, for example sperm ones, are attacked by self immune system quite often. Low titers of antispermal autoantibodies are common for healthy men (K. Tung, 1975). It is even suggested (P.-E. Bigazzi, 1983), that such autoreactive Ig may take part in natural regulation of spermatogenesis. At the same time, antisperm autoallergy with high titers of antisperm Ig is a common reason for masculine infertility, e.g. it exists in many chronic alcohol abusers (up to 60% of tested patients in D. von Tiel’s investigation of 1977). Nazrul-Islam and co-workers (1982) have demonstrated that antispermal allergy can be induced also in women, especially if the number of sexual partners is large. This is risky for immunological infertility in females. All this findings indirectly confirm the idea that the absence of contact between immature lymphocytes and certain antigens favors the autoimmune responses.

Clonal deletion of autoreactive T-cells in thymus occurs because normally thymic D-cells and, maybe also thymic epithelial and lymphoid cells) produce and present (in individual manner, controlled by self-MHC haplotype) to developing thymocytes various oligopeptides, corresponding to the whole repertoire of own sequential epitopes. This repertoire is not endless, because it includes quite limited number of oligomeric combinations, peculiar to the biopolimeres of this individual, but not the measureless diversity of conformational determinants. CD8+ cytotoxic cells express self-antigens in the context of MHC 1st class glycoproteins and eliminate the autoreactive immature cells (veto-effect). Thus, the overwhelming majority of autoreactive T cells perish in thymus (clonal deletion or break of cloning), so peripheral lymphoid organs contain just occasional few autoreactive T helper/inductor cells and autoreactive T killer/suppressor cells, which commonly “keep silence” due to post-thymic toleration mechanisms (clonal anergy). The post-thymic mechanisms aimed at the control of T-helper behavior and include anti-idiotypic, antigen-specific and non-specific suppression (I. Roitt , J. Miller 1994).

On the contrary, autoreactive B-lymphocytes do not proceed through this intrathymic step in their differentiation. They are not eliminated and normally live in blood and in tissues in considerable amount! E.g. S. Hoch et al. have revealed the same number of autoreactive B clones both in healthy persons and in SLE patients (1983). Moreover, new autoreactive B-clones may be elicited after birth, as a result of somatic mutation, or acquire autoreactive potential after penetration of cross-reacting exogenous antigens (H. Wigzell, 1988). But, not getting an effective help from silent T-clones and being under the action of direct suppression (clonal deletion of cells, which meet membrane-associated self antigens in bone marrow and tissues, clonal anergy of circulating cells, achieved through the influence of anti-idiotypes and circulating monomer autoantigens), autoreactive B cells normally establish very small titers of autoantibodies, adequate for the purposes of autoimmune regulation, but not sufficient for autoallergic tissue damage. Self-tolerance is facilitated by relative isolation and sequestration of extrathymic antigens, as well as by normal absence of MHC II glycoproteins and costimulatory molecules on the majority of non-stimulated somatic cells. Some immunosuppressive hormones and cytokines increase the threshold of autoreactive cell response.

But, the main mechanism of suppression is via anti-idiotypic autoantibodies towards self-reacting clones (I. Urbain, 1984). It means that autoallergy is prevented by… autoimmunity itself!

The self-tolerance is not absolutely strict. It is common finding to reveal autoantibodies in a normal healthy individual. Thus, the simple fact of their presence in low titers is not the equivalent of disease. There are different concepts on the significance of physiological autoantibodies.

One of the most original views regards them as natural regulating signals, able to alter cell growth and genetically determined cellular functions (along with or instead of hormones, neurotransmitters and other autocoids).

As it was discussed earlier (see: Non-destructive mechanisms of cytotoxic reactions), the autoantibodies against cell receptors, hormones and enzymes, as well as auto-Ig against cys-elements of chromatin, may act in very small titers, as signals of cell communication and render regulatory effects on target cells both under normal and pathological conditions. Due to this, physiological autoantibodies against endocrine cells, hormones and nuclear antigens are found especially frequently.

The natural autoantibodies may serve as blocking or stimulating signal for certain receptor-mediated cellular functions and even de-repress the transcription of some genetic programs. It has been shown (A.Sh. Zaichick, L.P. Churilov, 1981-1988), that antinuclear antibodies in vivo are capable to stimulate DNA and RNA biosynthesis within target cells, penetrating into living cell nuclei ( ref. to fig. ). M. Gross and R. Redman have demonstrated the possibility to alter translation in reticulocyte lysate by means of specific antibodies (1986). Growth stimulating and thyroid function stimulating autoantiboidies are revealed not only in Graves’ disease and other thyropathies, but also in apparently healthy individuals (T. Risdall et al., 1983, R. Brown et al. 1984). Similar stimulating autoantibodies are found in adrenal cortex disorders (D. Petranyi, 1983).

According to the opinion of P.N. Grabar (1963, 1975), the autoantibodies may alter the regulation of cellular functions via non-direct pathway: through autoantigen elimination and transportation. N.V. Il’chevich et al. believe that enzymatic and anti-enzymatic action of autoantibodies are of great physiological significance (1988). These authors have experimentally produced the catalitic antiidiotypic autoantibodies against some liver oxidative enzymes. Recently S. Paul et al. (1997) discovered thyroglobulin cleaving proteolytic activity in some autoantibodies from SLE and Hashimoto’s thyroiditis patients’ sera.

A variety of these concepts is a view promoted by several authors, associating natural autoantibodies with regulation of tissue morphogenesis. M.M.B. Kay and J. Marchalonis described the natural non-tissue speciphic antigen-marker of senescent cells (band III protein), targeted by natural antibodies, serving as the agents of programmed cell death (1983). P. Vertosick and R. Kelly hypothesized, that natural autoantibodies regulate the switch of cell differentiation stages and pathways (1983). In later papers A.G. Babayeva et al. (1986) regarded autoreactive natural immunoglobulins as stimulators of both physiological and reparative regeneration. The existense of organ speciphic mitogenic autoantibodies was proven for the process of liver regeneration. Perhaps, the most inordinate approach was proclaimed by N.N. Klemparskaya and G.A. Shal’nova (1978) who has found some radioprotective abilities in natural autoantibodies.

Not only inflammatory, but also disregulatory autoimmune diseases are provoked by relatively high titers of autoantibodies. Normally autoreactive Igs present in significantly lower titers, so their effect does not replace or overcome the action of other normal regulators (hormones and neurotransmitters), but just supplements with them.

The phenomenon of physiological autoimmunity is still far from complete explanation. Nevertheless, it has been clearly demonstrated that autoantibodies may display regulating effects over target self cells via surface receptors or after their internalization – binding various intracellular and intranuclear regulatory triggers. There are many interesting facts strongly supporting the idea of self-regulation via autoimmunity. M.Norcross (1984) has demonstrated that Thy-1 surface lymphocyte antigen, taking part in intercellular immune cooperation, is identical to one of CNS synaptic proteins. J.E. Blalock have regarded the immune system as sensory one, responsible for the “sense of antigenicity”. He has also proven that the lymphocytes triggered by cytokines produce neurohormones (growth hormone, prolactin, TSH, MSH, ACTH and opiates). In parallel, the cytokine-like effects of neuropeptides and stimulation of hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal axis by means of IL-1, IL-6 and other lymphocyte products was discovered (M. Svennilssen, C. Rivier et al 1989, 1992).

All these facts are used to expand the concept of physiological autoimmunity and include the immune system, along with nervous system and endocrine system, into so called Communicative Regulatory Integrative Apparatus (CRIA) – the term coined by L.G. Leybson in 1984. Judging from this point, not only abnormal increase of autoimmune response, but also pathological decrease or absense of certain autoimmune reactions may cause autoimmune diseases. But, traditionally only excessive autoimmunity is regarded as autoallergy.

An alternative point of view interprets natural autoantibodies as a phenomenon of no (or non-significant) physiological importance. According R. Schwartz et al (1986), P.Casali; A. Notkins (1989), these naturally occurring autoantibodies are directed against most common autoantigens (DNA, thyroglobulin, myelin, hormones and their receptors, Fc-fragments, heat shock proteins), which have epitope structure, close to highly spread exoantigens. Natural autoreactive Igs belong to M class and are produced on initial stages of primary immune response by special subset of CD5-positive B-cells (constituted for almost 20% of all B cells). Their variable chain domens are encoded for germ cell line genes Vn3-n5 and may be regarded as rudiments of some ancient less selective mechanisms of primary immune response.

According to this theory, autoallergy may occur due to boosting of their expression.

Although this concept seems to be true for auto-IgM of low affinity, it fails to explain the significance of highly affine IgG autoantibodies, which also are registered in small titers under normal conditions.

Anyway, the facts about natural autoimmunity witness for relative character of self-tolerance.
