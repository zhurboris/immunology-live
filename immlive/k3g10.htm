GENETIC BACKGROUND OF ANAPHYLAXIS

There is no universal genetic mechanism, predisposing an individual simultaneously to all different kinds of allergy. Every particular category of hypersensitivity has distinct concrete genetic background. A person inclined to anaphylaxis may be absolutely non-risky for immune complex disorders and vise versa.

Familiar predisposition to anaphylaxis is based on multifactorial mode of inheritance and controlled by several genes, acting in additive way with threshold effect, related to some external and internal conditions.

The predisposed individuals develop more intensive IgE production under the less intensity of allergenic offense. The rate of concordance in monozygous twins is 60%. If both parents suffer from anaphylaxis, the risk for children is estimated as 50%, in one parent involved – about 30%. The ability to respond for allergens with anaphylaxis is controlled, first of all, by genes of MHC-complex. Besides them, intensity of reagin antibody biosynthesis depends of variety of genes coded for all regulators and receptors aforementioned. It has been shown, that the cytokines, driving the immune response to either anaphylactic or normergic route, are encoded in 5th chromosome (locus 5q31.1). Of greatest importance for the risk of anaphylaxis is the degree of CD40 expression on B-lymphocytes surface. Shames and Adelman (1997) believe that this marker can account for reception of a signal(s) triggering IgE synthesis. The "hyperreactors" (or persons, predisposed to anaphylaxis) may have various genetically caused backgrounds for their specific reactivity:

  • the effectiveness of IL-4, IL-5, IL-6, IL-13 elevated,
  • glycosylation of Fce - receptor soluble fragments accelerated,
  • kininogens conversion and kinins action increased,
  • secretion and effectiveness of IFNa / g declined,
  • suppressive functions of glucocorticoids and/or lipomodulin insufficient, macrophagal IL-12 production depressed,
  • responsiveness of H2/H3 histaminic receptors decreased,
  • Permeability of tissue barriers for allergens increased
  • Eotaxin response exaggerated etc.

At the same time, it is well known that spontaneously low production of IgE in humans depend on action of single dominant gene. Although there are no significant associations between MHC antigens and atopic diseases as a group, significant association between some MHC loci and immune responses against certain allergens has been noted.

Due to this, in low total IgE production, the isolated reagin response for certain minor epitopes may broadly vary, depending on MHC class 1 and class 2 glycoproteins. For example, anaphylaxis towards ragweed (Ambrosia) is related to alleles A2, B7, D1-2 and especially – to allele B8 (K. Brostoff, T. Hall, 1994). The last allele is related to suppressive function of lymphocytes and correlates also with high risk of cytotoxic reactions. . MHC-D2 allele is associated with strong IgE-inducing properties of ragweed allergen 5. Furthermore, IgE antibody production to some pollen extracts has been correlated with specific HLA haplotypes in certain families.

Additive multigene character of anaphylaxis risk inheritance mode is reflected in "allergic breakthrough concept".

According to this concept, the realization of an anaphylactic reaction requires besides the presence of allergen and existence of particular reactivity also certain nonspecific threshold of general immune activity to be overwhelmed. Nonspecific factors: polyclonal immune stimulators, superantigens, all agents decreasing the suppressor activity and/of IgA biosynthesis, increasing the permeability of natural barriers, augmenting the histamine production and inhibiting the adenylatecyclases - may facilitate the overcome of this threshold and serve as universal impetus for the reaction caused by the specific allergen.

The classic non-specific provocateurs of various allergic reactions are EBV, herpes virus, some air pollutants (nitric and sulfur oxides, cigarette smoke, diesel fuel decay products). All of them are not anaphylactogenic per se, but may facilitate the manifestation of asthmatic bronchitis, caused by many other specific allergens and/or by non-allergic neuroendocrine mechanisms. This occurs because of allergic breakthrough effect, adjuvant action and/or damage of bronchial mucosa, produced by the provocateurs.

With this background of the mechanisms involved in type I hypersensitivity and its genetic background, we can turn to its concrete systemic and local clinical manifestations.
