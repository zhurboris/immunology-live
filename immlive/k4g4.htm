THE KEY LINK OF AUTOALLERGY PATHOGENESIS

There are 2 main pathways for disorder of self-tolerance (L. Jeger, 1989). Everyone has several varieties and illustrated in clinics by many examples.

  • The autoallergy may be initiated due to disinhibition of autoreactive T-helper activity or failure of their deletion. As a result, autoreactive helpers promote the activation of existing (normally, silent) autoreactive effector clones (this situation is known as bypass of regulatory mechanisms).
  • The autoallergy may be induced via bypass of autoreactive T helpers, even if they are eliminated or anergic. In this case autoreactive effectors are stimulated without any participation of anti-self helpers, by another helpers or by another direct mechanisms.

The 1st mechanism (bypass of regulatory mechanisms) acts in several different situations:

  • in deficiency of suppressing influences
  • in abnormal expression of MHC Class II glycoproteins and/or costimulatory molecules on the cells, which normally do not express them.
  • In spontaneous activation of silent autoreactive T-helpers

The 2nd mechanism occurs in:

  • Direct stimulation of B-cells by superantigens or polyclonal immunostimulators.
  • Different forms of cross-reactivity (molecular mimicry) when autoreactive B-lymphocyte is triggered by anti-alien helper, by idiotype-specific helper, or if antiidiotypic antibody idiotype interacts with autoantigen (I. Cohen, 1989; I. Roitt, 1984)- see also fig. ).

Previously the dominating opinion about the key mechanism of autoallergy was different. According classical concepts, exposure of “hidden” autoantigens, cloistered by some barriers from the immature lymphocytes, was evaluated as one of the leading causes for autoallergy.

Thus, emergence of a sequestered antigen means, that any self-antigen that is completely sequestered is likely to be viewed as foreign if introduced into circulation, and an immune response will develop. Spermatozoa, myelin basic protein, and lens crystalin may fall into this category of sequestered antigens.

The last decades brought many new facts related to this aspect of the problem. It seems now, that real amount of hidden autoantigens is very limited and contribution of this mechanism into the pathogenesis of autoallergy was a little bit overestimated, although it should not be neglected at all.

Traditionally, the autoantigens of lens, testis, CNS, thyroid colloid, adrenals - were regarded as hidden ones. Hence, violation of natural barriers, sequestering them from immune cells, by trauma, by poisoning, or in course of infectious activation of endothelium - was blamed for sympathetic ophthalmitis, autoimmune orchitis, allergic encephalomyelitis, autoimmune thyroiditis and similar states.

With no doubts, it is true that injury of blood-tissue barriers and expression of endothelial cell adhesion molecules facilitates the migration of immune effectors into altered organs and accelerates the development of autoimmune tissue damage. There are appropriate experimental and clinical examples (e.g., traumatic orchitis – N. Rose, 1983).

But, there are also many recent data, proving that principally, in normal organism, virtually all autoantigens are prone for immunological surveillance, so the contribution of “disclosure” mechanism in the whole spectrum of autoallergy in humans, probably, is minimal (R. Cotran et al, 1994).

The main of these facts are as follows:

  • Sensitive immunodiagnostical procedures (ELISA, RIA) revealed in peripheral blood and other biological fluids of healthy individuals immunologically significant amounts of the autoantigens, previously regarded as “hidden” ones, for example, thyreoglobulin and myelinic basic protein (I. Roitt, 1984; P. Patherson, E.D. Day, 1981-1982). This does not necessarily provoke autoimmune thyroiditis or encephalomyelitis.
  • Blood-brain barrier (BBB – entity a lot spoken about!) was proven to be much more restricting for penetration of macromolecules from bloodstream into CNS, than for reverse transfer from CNS into bloodstream, which pathway is many times more “permeable” (J. B. Aarli, 1983). Moreover, BBB is not a border between CNS and immune system. Such a statement is totally wrong. There are local immune systems behind BBB and behind other blood-tissue barriers. They are composed by local APCs and local effectors, for example, within CNS and BBB itself we may find astrocytes, able to present antigens, and intrathecal lymphocytes, capable to render immunological effects (A.S. Shevelyov, 1986, 1991).
  • In the 90-ies the concepts on the immune response for CNS autoantigens (for a long time thought to be absolutely isolated from immune system) were entirely revised. One of the founders of neurobiology, Spanish scientist P. Del Rio-Ortega prophetically insisted on macrophageal origin of microglial cells (1932). Nowadays, it is obvious that microglial cells are very close to macrophages, although have different bone marrow precursors. They express MHC antigens of Class 1, and (upon viral or cytokine stimulation) also Class II ( V.J. Straight, K. Kincade-Colton, 1995; B. Zweiman, A.I. Levinson). Microglial cells produce IL-1, IL-3, IL-6 and TNFa and can process and present CNS autoantigens to local intrathecal lymphocytes. Via these cytokines microglia is able to activate endotheliocytes of BBB, trigger the expression of cell adhesion molecules and increase the BBB permeability for lymphoid cells. Normally these processes have moderate intensity, but their exaggeration may cause autoallergic involvement of CNS (which is observed in multiple sclerosis, lateral amyotrophic sclerosis and other similar diseases).
  • On the contrary to the classic concept of intracellular antigens isolation (W. Boyd, 1969), the experimental and clinical laboratory proving was established for the idea of intracellular penetration of immunoglobulins (D. Alarcon-Segovia et al. 1973-1983; A.S. Zaichik et al. 1973-1991) and emperipolesis of lymphocytes into living cells (J.P. Sandilands et al. 1978; I.J. Fidler, 1980).

Hence, as it was stated by S.Robbins et al (1989): “Seductive as this (antigen sequestration) theory may be, it is probably not a major mechanism of autoimmune reactioms in humans and is applicable only to the special situations cited”.

Now, we shall turn to the discussion of main concrete mechanisms of autoallergy – straight in the order listed above.
