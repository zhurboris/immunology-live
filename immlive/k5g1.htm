PRIMARY IMMUNODEFICIENCIES

The primary immunodeficiencies are genetically determined and may affect specific immunity (i.e., humoral and cellular) or nonspecific host defense mechanisms mediated by complement proteins and cells such as phagocytes or NK cells. Most primary immunodeficiencies manifest themselves in infancy, between six months and two years of life, and they are noted because of the susceptibility of infants to recurrent infections.
