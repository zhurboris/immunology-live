REGULATION AND DYSREGULATION THROUGH ANTI-RECEPTOR AUTOANTIBODIES

The existence of non-destroying sequels of autoantibody action over target cells is now a proven fact. The Ig towards cell surface and chromatin receptors may cause signal effects. Due to their relatively low titers and because of immediate internalization, they do not trigger extracellular lytic effector systems, but instead alter cell growth and/or functions. In experiments many research groups have obtained anti-receptor or anti-nuclear antibodies, partially or entirely imitating in target cells the biological effects of prolactin (J. Gianne et al.; 1981), epidermal growth factors (D. Gospodarowisz, 1983), insulin and vasopressin (T. Railly, R. Root, 1986; I.Cohen, Y. Shechter, 1988), ACTH, TSH and hypothalamic liberins (A.Sh. Zaichik et al. 1969-1988, H. Luddens et al. 1982), insulin-like growth factors (J. Hari et al. 1987) and some other autocoids and hormones. Blocking effects are equally possible for the signal Ig, which are suitable enough to bind a receptor, but not apt to substitute the post-receptor effect of a natural ligand. All clinical examples of such stimulating (e.g. Graves’ disease) and blocking effects (e.g. myasthenia gravis) belong to autoimmune diseases, that’s why they will be considered later in the section “Autoimmunity and Autoallergy”. Here we describe only one similar example from the field of asthmology. P. Couraud et al. (1983) have shown that one of the possible mechanisms of bronchial asthma and allergic rhinitis, not related to anaphylaxis, is so called pathoreceptor mechanism. In some patients the cause of these phenomena are blocking antibodies towards bronchial b 2 - adrenergic receptors. They may arise spontaneously (in this case patients suffer from allergic rhinitis without reaginic Ig hyperproduction and have no pollinosis). More common is the situation when these autoantibodies are induced by treatment of an asthmatic patient with frequent and excessive inhalations of b - adrenergic broncholytics as a kind of monotherapy in attacks of classic bronchial asthma. Anti-receptor autoantibodies are produced as auto-antiidiotypic Ig against antibodies towards drugs. The pathogenetic role of these anti-receptor autoantibodies in the mechanism of the most dangerous complication of treated bronchial asthma – asthmatic status – is clearly proven. The autoantibodies cause the resistance of patients with asthmatic status to broncholytic drugs. By the way, before the era of b - adrenergic portable inhalators the asthmatic statuses were almost not known and the lethal cases in asthmatic attacks were occasional. As it was demonstrated by I.N. Alexeeva et al (1987) anti-idiotypic antibodies may even replace the function of antigens, which carry enzymatic function, for example liver cytochromes. In our opinion, the term “cytotoxic” is not applicable to the effects of this kind. They can be designated as “cytoregulating reactions” and for their pathological manifestation we recommend the term: “dysregulatory reactions”. The whole diversity of effects caused by anti-cellular Ig can be designated by the term: “ cytokinetic reactions”.
