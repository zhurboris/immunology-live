IMMUNODEFICIENCIES

At the sunrise of Medicine, the physicians of Ancient epoch have noticed that maladies, pests and fevers spare some happy persons, but visit others very often and come to the same person with dozens. The Bible texts keep the story of miserable Job who was punished by Lord with multiple sores and ulcers.

A child who frequently falls ill and stays sick for prolonged time is an eternal problem of Paediatrics. Even at the beginning of immunological era (1911), M. Pfaundler wrote, that the "reason for this constant relapsing illnesses is very often some internal rediness for diseases, not poor care or improper diet".

In the 50-ies and 60-ies of the 20-th century it has been revealed that the ontogenesis of immune system may be altered, giving start to congenital or acquired deviations, manifested in failure of immune responses. These disorders were called immunodeficits or immune deficiencies. As primary immune deficiencies are quite rare, for a long time the whole problem was located somewhere in peripheral area of practical immunologist's interests, drawing mostly the attention of theoretics, because this syndromes are experiments of nature that document the complexities inherent in man's immune system. Nowhere has the relevance of the individual components of immunologic function been more distinctly shown than when deficiencies of single components have given rise to distinctive disorders. Indeed, many of the important concepts of immunology either arose from or were confirmed by the study of clinical examples of specific immunodeficiencies.

But, as soon as real prevalence of acquired immune deficiencies started to be recognized, and, especially after medical registration of HIV-infection and AIDS syndrome, the attitude to this problem entirely changed and health care community understood, that mankind lives under the progressing pandemic of acquired immune deficits. Totally, over 100 of new cases of various immune deficiencies are registered every week, with predominance of that caused by HIV-infection. Millions of people are living in state of permanent acquired immune deficit due to ecological, dietary and infectious causes. So, immune deficiency is now the hottest problem of health care and a frontier of medical research.

Traditionally, immunodeficiency disorders are considered according to the primary component or components involved (i.e., the B cell, the T cell, the undifferentiated stem cell, or complement), however, in view of the major cell interactions between T and B lymphocytes and macrophages, these distinctions are not always clear-cut.

The individuals with immunoglobulin production defects, complement and phagocytic cell disorders suffer from recurrent pyogenic bacterial infection, especially with encapsulated bacteria. If T-cell link of immunity is altered, this is manifested in viral, fungi and persistent intracellular parasites infections as well as increased tumor incidence. Autoimmune disorders may accompany various types of immunodeficits.

Immunodeficiencies can be divided into the primary immunodeficiency disorders, which are almost always genetically determined, and secondary immunodeficiency states, arising as complications of infections, malnutrition, aging, or side effects of immunosuppression, irradiation, or chemotherapy for cancer and other autoimmune diseases. Both primary and secondary immunodeficiency disorders are being discovered with increasing frequency because of new laboratory methods that can detect various cellular and humoral components of the immunologic reaction.

Here we briefly discuss some of the more important primary immunodeficiencies with phagocytosis and complement disorders staying beyond the scope of our attention, as they belong to non-specific part of host response. The discussion of primary and various secondary immunodeficits is followed by a more detailed description of the acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS), the most devastating example of secondary immunodeficiency.

To the end of XX century the following classification of immune deficits was formulated:

1. Primary immunodeficiencies:

1.1. B-cell defects

  • X-linked agammaglobulinemia – Bruton's disease
  • IgA deficiency
  • IgG subclasses deficiency
  • Ig deficiency with IgM excess
  • Common variable immunodeficiency
  • Transient neonatal hypogammaglobulinemia
  • Hypogammaglobulinemia in Bloom's syndrome
  • IgA secretory component deficit
  • Deficiency of Ig and transcobalamine II
  • X-linked lymphoproliferative immunopathy
  • Ig κ-chain deficiency

1.2. T-cell defects

  • Thymic Hypoplasia (DiGeorge's syndrome)
  • T-lymphopenia (Thymic dysplasia, Nezelof's syndrome)
  • Purinenucleozidephosphorylase (PNP) deficiency
  • Cartilage-hair dysplasia (Immunodeficit of short-legged dwarfism)
  • Chronic cutaneomuscular candidiasis

1.3. Combined immunodeficiencies

  • Autosomal recessive severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID, Swiss-type and ZAP-70 – dependent type)
  • Adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficit
  • X-linked SCID
  • Bare lymphocyte syndrome and other defects of surface cell molecules
  • Dysgenesia reticularis (Vaal-Seynhaeve syndrome)
  • Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome
  • Ataxia-teleangiectasia
  • Immunodeficiency with thymoma
  • Job's syndrome
  • Entheropathic acrodermatitis
  • Mixed immunodeficits in other familiar diseases

2. Secondary immunodeficiencies:

  • 2.1. Infectious and parasitic (except AIDS)
  • 2.2. Chemical and drug-induced
  • 2.3. In autoallergy
  • 2.4. In non-infectious primary disorders
  • 2.5. Caused by diet
  • 2.6. Caused by HIV-infection
