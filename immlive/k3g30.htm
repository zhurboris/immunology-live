TISSUE DAMAGE IN DTH

In some examples of type IV hypersensitivity tissue damage is prominent (graft rejection, viral infections). In others it is not so massive, although present in the centers of all granulomas. Due to different extent of damage, some authors insist on two variants of type IV process and prefer to call those with marked damage “ T-cell mediated cytotoxicity” and others “ delayed type hypersensitivity”. But, in our opinion both cell damage and exudative components of inflammation, of course, present in any inflammation focus. The difference is relative and quantitative. The main CD8+ lymphocyte dependent cytotoxic mechanisms involved in alterative component of DTH inflammation is the following:

  1. Pore formation in target cell membrane followed by permeability changes that lead to osmotic death; according to this schema, granules within the cytoplasm of cytotoxic T cells contain pore-forming proteins (perforins, cytolysins) that bind to the target cell membrane and assemble into pores or channels (5-20 nm in diameter), similar to those produced by the membrane attack complex of complement; calcium enters into the cell, causing its necrosis or apoptosis.
  2. Release of apoptosis triggering molecules (Fas ligands) such as TNFb (lymphotoxin) and TNFa (cachexin) that may cause the target cell death, binding its Fas molecules.
  3. Release of proteolytic enzymes, especially serine esterases that may damage the cell membrane directly or activate precursors of other cytotoxic molecules.

The detailed discussion of these mechanisms can be found in th section of “Basic Immunology.” Here it is necessary to stress, that CD8+ mediated cytolysis is highly specific and that adjacent ("innocent bystander") cells are not damaged. Critical to such specificity is the ability of cytotoxic lymphocytes (CTLs) to recognize the cells bearing the appropriate target antigens. It is now abundantly clear that class I MHC antigen play an extremely important role in the process by which CD8+CTLs recognized their target cells. They present to CTLs various endocellular, first of all - tumor and viral peptides. Moreover, CTLs, directed against MHC antigens themselves, are central effectors of transplant rejection. CTL generated during a virus infection can recognize the virus- infected cell only in association with self cell-surface MHC antigen, as discussed previously. The lysis of virus-infected cells by CTL prior to the synthesis of new virions can lead in due course to elimination of the viral infection. Tumor antigens expressed on the cancer cells may also be viewed as modified class I antigens, thus evoking a CTL response that may contribute to immunosurveillance. CD8+ cells also produce cytokines, and their IFNg is very potent stimulating signal for cell cytotoxicity and inhibiting – for virus replication.
