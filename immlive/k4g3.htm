GENETIC PREDISPOSITION TO AUTOALLERGY

There are clear evidences of multifactorial genetic predisposition for autoallergy. Many strains of different laboratory animals were obtained (rats, mices, chickens, beagles and american minks) spontaneously developing organospecific and non-organospecific disorders in due time.

The most broadly known are OS chickens, developing spontaneous autoimmune thyroiditis and mice strains NZB/NZW and MRL, inclined to inevitably occurring lupus. According to E.I. Panteleyev (1979) autoimmune thyroid disorders, nephropathies, autoallergic encephalomyelitis and lupus-related syndromes are most prone to modelling in genetic strains of animals.

The clinical and anamnaestic findings obtained from the patients with autoallergic diseases also witness for great role of individual genetic predisposition to autoallergy. The same exogenous provocateurs of autoallergy (e.g. viral diabetogens) do precipitate the disease in some individuals and families, but are non-effective in others. There is some relation of organospecific autoallergy to anomalies of X-chromosome.

But the brightest and most carefully investigated genetic parallel exists between the relative risk of autoallergy and expression of some MHC alleles (J. Batchelor, H. Matej; 1984, A. Roitt; 1994).

There is familial clustering of many autoimmune disorders and their linkage with MHC, especially class II antigens (see above: Practical significance of MHC antigens.).

For instance, the patients with ankylosing spondilitis have B27 allele of MHC 90 times more often, than it occurs in general population. The haplotype DR3/B8 is typical for autoallergy towards cell receptors and for many organospecific autoimmune diseases. The carriage of DR3 increases the risk of Addison’s disease 3-fold. DR4 allele is associated both with high risk of organospecific autoimmune disorders (insulin dependent diabetes mellitus, autoimmune thyropathies, pemphigus) and with increased probability of non-organospecific rheumatoid arthritis. The allele DR5 is linked to higher incidence of autoimmune atrophic gastritis and pernicious anemia, autoimmune aplastic cytopeniae, autoimmune thyropathies and Still’s syndrome. Fore more concrete examples a reader may return to above mentioned section of the book. Here it is necessary to emphasize once more, that certain MHC haplotype does not make an autoimmune disorder inescapable. Because of necessity in exogenous antigenic or haptenic provocation, MHC set just creates some likely possibility of disease. We may say, that the distinction of self and alien peptides is more difficult for certain individuals, in context of their unique MHC glycoproteins. Perhaps, autoantiidiotypic response is too strong and/or poorly regulated in carriers of some MHC haplotypes. These or similar mechanisms may stand behind well-known linkage of many MHC alleles and autoallergic disorders.

The last breakthrough in this field is the demonstration of autoimmune disease (ankylosing spondylitis) induction in transgenic rats by transfer of cloned HLA-B27 gene (V.Kumar et al. 1997).
